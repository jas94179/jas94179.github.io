The challenge

Fast AI drafts are useless if experts can't trust them

Consultants spend hours on repeatable first drafts: interview guides, project charters, analysis summaries. AI can write those drafts in seconds, but in high-stakes deal work, a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer.

How might we help consulting teams move faster with AI while staying sure of every finding they share with a client?
Who it's for

Experts who are accountable for every word

Consultants

Need a strong first draft

Want to skip the blank page and spend their time on thinking, not formatting.

Reviewers

Need to know what to check

Can't re-read everything. Need the risky findings to stand out.

Client teams

Need client-ready output

Need summaries in the formats they already send, without reworking them.

One system, not eight products

Four patterns every tool shares

Instead of designing eight separate tools, I designed a small set of patterns and used them everywhere. Learn one tool, and you know them all.

Pattern 1

Guided inputs, ready-to-edit drafts

Consultants don't start from an empty prompt. They answer a few structured questions, and the tool returns a first draft they can edit.

  • Plain fields instead of a blank prompt
  • The draft is always editable, never final
  • Regenerate one section, not the whole thing

Pattern 2

Conversational Q&A over findings

Teams ask questions in plain words and get answers that point back to the documents they came from.

  • Every answer shows its sources
  • Follow-up questions keep the context
  • "I couldn't find this" instead of a guess

Pattern 3

Inline confidence and review states

AI output arrives with a confidence level. Low-confidence findings are flagged, so reviewers know exactly where to look first.

  • High, Medium and Low, never colour alone
  • Low confidence asks for a review
  • Progress shows what's still unchecked

Pattern 4

Client-ready export

Once findings are reviewed, the summary exports in the formats consultants already send to clients.

  • Only reviewed findings go into the export
  • Familiar formats, no reformatting
  • Who reviewed what stays on record
Design principles

The rules behind the patterns

01

Drafts, not decisions

AI writes the first version. People own the final one.

02

Show the source

Every finding points back to where it came from.

03

Make doubt visible

Confidence levels tell reviewers where to look first.

04

Consistent everywhere

Same patterns across all eight tools.

What I learned

Trust is the real feature

Experts trust AI they can check

Sources and confidence mattered more to users than speed alone.

Patterns scale, screens don't

A few shared patterns let eight tools feel like one product.

Review is part of the flow

When review is a built-in step, people actually do it.
